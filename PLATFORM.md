# Page Purpose

The Platform page explains how TULBOXX works as one connected operating system for a service business.

This is the first page in the website journey where the product becomes the primary proof.

The page should show how customers, projects, estimates, jobs, invoices, payments, and communication remain connected as work moves forward. It should help the visitor understand the system without turning into a feature catalog or dashboard gallery.

## Editorial Ownership

This page owns the connected-information model: one customer, one job, and one continuous history across the work. It should prove relationships between information, not re-teach the product philosophy, narrate every workflow step, or inventory every feature.

# Section 3 - Bring Information Into the Project

## Stage

Understanding

## Question Answered

How does the first information become part of the connected record?

## Copy

**Eyebrow**

Give the first details one place to belong.

**Headline**

A call, photo, or supported note becomes project context.

**Body copy**

Bring the customer details, property information, photos, measurements, and supported notes into the same project. As more is learned, it joins the existing record instead of creating another disconnected starting point.

## Input Examples

- Customer call summary
- Text or message
- Property and job photos
- Voice description
- Rough scope notes
- Address and contact information
- Measurements and attachments

## Visual Direction

**Asset ID:** `PLATFORM-CAPTURE-03`

**Purpose**

Show how confirmed input becomes part of the connected project record.

**Format**

A short field-to-platform sequence.

**Sequence**

1. The contractor captures a voice note and property photos.
2. The information appears inside the demonstration project.
3. The contractor adds or corrects the details that matter.
4. The project now has a usable starting point.

**Product requirement**

The final product state must visibly distinguish original information from any organized or suggested content when the product does so.

**Avoid**

- Perfectly completed forms appearing instantly
- Implied automatic decisions
- Generated text inside photography
- Claims about input channels not confirmed in the product

## Exit Condition

The visitor should think:

> I can see where the information goes and what it stays connected to.

# Section 8 - The Record Grows With the Work

## Stage

Can the connected record hold the right level of detail for different work?

## Copy

**Eyebrow**

Connected without being overloaded.

**Headline**

Keep the record focused, then add what the job requires.

**Body copy**

A straightforward job may need only the customer, work, completion, and payment. A larger project may add approved scope, phases, documentation, communication, and billing detail while preserving the same connected history.

## Editorial Boundary

Why TULBOXX owns the progressive-structure philosophy. This section should prove its effect on the platform's information model.

## Comparison

### Straightforward Job

- Customer and address
- Short work description
- Schedule or immediate service
- Simple completion and payment path

### Larger Project

- Detailed scope and attachments
- Estimate revisions and approvals
- Multiple contacts and scheduled phases
- Change documentation
- Deposits or progress billing when supported
- Extended communication history

- Show the larger-project path revealing additional structure as needed.
- Keep optional complexity visually secondary until selected.

**Verification rule**

Every larger-project capability shown here must be confirmed in the current product. Unsupported examples remain roadmap context and must not appear in launch copy.

# Visual System for This Page

## Canonical Reference

Use [`docs/TULBOXX_BRAND_STYLE_GUIDE.pdf`](docs/TULBOXX_BRAND_STYLE_GUIDE.pdf) for brand colors, typography, logo treatment, and overall visual character.

## Page Character

The Platform page should feel:

- Product-led but human
- Organized rather than technical
- Capable without appearing complex
- Connected rather than modular
- Calm enough that the product feels learnable

## Product-to-Photography Ratio

Use more product UI than on Home or Why TULBOXX, but keep real work present.

Recommended rhythm:

1. Product-led hero
2. Product relationship explanation
3. Real-world capture moment
4. Product workflow proof
5. Product priority view
6. Contractor and customer views
7. Product AI detail
8. Product flexibility comparison
9. Platform summary
10. Product-led CTA

## Product UI Rules

- Use current approved screens as the source of truth.
- Use one fictional demonstration project throughout.
- Keep visible UI text readable.
- Crop to the action being explained.
- Show the contractor's action when the product requires one.
- Never imply automation, connections, or states that do not exist.
- Do not redesign product UI solely for a cleaner marketing image without product approval.

## Photography Rules

- Photography shows where information begins and why the system matters.
- Use credible contractors, customers, tools, properties, trucks, and job conditions.
- Product UI should be composited into devices after the photography is approved.
- Never ask an image model to create product screens or readable paperwork.
- Generated people must not be presented as real customers.

## Motion Rules

- Motion explains connection, continuity, or progress.
- The same project identity should anchor every transition.
- Avoid floating screens with no spatial or narrative relationship.
- Do not animate merely to make the product seem fast.
- Provide clear static states and respect reduced-motion preferences.

# Mobile Experience

- Introduce the central project before revealing connected records.
- Show one relationship or workflow step at a time.
- Keep the demonstration project label visible during sequences.
- Never shrink a desktop dashboard until it becomes unreadable.
- Use focused mobile captures where the mobile product is the relevant experience.
- Keep CTA placement predictable after major explanatory sections.

---

# Accessibility Requirements

- Use one semantic `h1` for the hero headline.
- Preserve logical heading hierarchy.
- Describe product relationships in text as well as visually.
- Do not communicate statuses through color alone.
- Provide meaningful alt text for informative product images.
- Make all interactive maps and sequences keyboard accessible.
- Announce state changes appropriately to assistive technology.
- Provide static alternatives for scroll-driven animation.
- Meet WCAG AA contrast at minimum.
- Respect reduced-motion and text-resizing preferences.

---

# Performance Requirements

- Prioritize the hero copy and initial central-project view.
- Load later product sequences only as they approach the viewport.
- Use focused image crops rather than full-screen captures where possible.
- Avoid embedding multiple autoplay videos.
- Reserve media dimensions to prevent layout shift.
- Test animation and interaction on mid-range mobile devices.
- Keep the platform understandable if rich media fails to load.
