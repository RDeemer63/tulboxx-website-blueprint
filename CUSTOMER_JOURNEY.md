# CUSTOMER_JOURNEY.md

# TULBOXX Customer Journey

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Canonical  
**Purpose:** Define the psychological journey every visitor should experience from their first impression of TULBOXX to becoming a customer.

---

# Introduction

People do not buy software because they understand every feature.

They buy because they believe the software will improve their future.

The purpose of the TULBOXX website is to guide visitors through a series of emotional and logical decisions.

Every page should answer one important question before naturally leading to the next.

This document defines that journey.

---

# The Journey

```
Unaware

↓

Recognition

↓

Curiosity

↓

Hope

↓

Understanding

↓

Belief

↓

Trust

↓

Confidence

↓

Decision

↓

Customer

↓

Advocate
```

Each stage has its own questions, emotions, and responsibilities.

---

# Stage 1

## Unaware

### What they're thinking

"I'm busy."

"I've got estimates to finish."

"I'll deal with software later."

They're not looking for features.

They're trying to survive another week.

### Our responsibility

Interrupt.

Not with software.

With empathy.

Help them recognize themselves.

---

# Stage 2

## Recognition

The visitor realizes...

"This website understands my business."

"This feels different."

"They know exactly what my day looks like."

They begin recognizing problems they've accepted as normal.

Examples:

Late-night estimates.

Forgotten follow-ups.

Paper everywhere.

Five disconnected apps.

Customers texting multiple phone numbers.

Nothing organized.

Recognition creates attention.

---

# Stage 3

## Curiosity

Now they want to know...

"What is this?"

"Why haven't I seen this before?"

"Is this another CRM?"

Our responsibility is not to answer everything.

Our responsibility is to earn another scroll.

Curiosity is fragile.

Never overwhelm it.

---

# Stage 4

## Hope

This is where transformation begins.

The visitor starts imagining...

"What if my business actually felt organized?"

"What if estimates weren't such a pain?"

"What if everything stayed connected?"

Hope is more valuable than information.

Protect it.

---

# Stage 5

## Understanding

Only now do we explain.

The visitor asks...

"How does this actually work?"

This is where:

Platform

Workflow

Screenshots

Animations

Features

Integrations

AI

begin appearing.

Not before.

---

# Stage 6

## Belief

Visitors begin replacing skepticism with confidence.

"I can see myself using this."

"This actually solves my problems."

"This isn't software built by software people."

Belief comes from:

Real workflows.

Real screenshots.

Real stories.

Real contractors.

Not marketing hype.

---

# Stage 7

## Trust

Trust is earned.

Never claimed.

We build trust through:

Testimonials.

Customer stories.

Transparent pricing.

Honest language.

Professional design.

Clear product philosophy.

Consistency.

Trust is the bridge between interest and action.

---

# Stage 8

## Confidence

At this point visitors believe:

"I know enough."

"I understand the value."

"I think this will help."

They no longer need convincing.

They simply need an easy next step.

Reduce friction.

Don't add it.

---

# Stage 9

## Decision

The decision should feel natural.

Not pressured.

Visitors should feel:

"I'm ready."

Not...

"I guess I'll try it."

That difference matters.

---

# Stage 10

## Customer

The website's job is not finished.

The customer's first experience should reinforce every promise made before signup.

Consistency matters.

Trust compounds.

---

# Stage 11

## Advocate

The ultimate goal isn't another customer.

It's someone who says:

"You need to check out TULBOXX."

That only happens when expectations and reality match.

Or exceed them.

---

# Questions Throughout The Journey

Every page should answer one important question.

| Stage | Question |
|---------|----------|
| Recognition | Do they understand my business? |
| Curiosity | What is TULBOXX? |
| Hope | Could this actually help me? |
| Understanding | How does it work? |
| Belief | Why is this different? |
| Trust | Can I rely on this company? |
| Confidence | Is this worth it? |
| Decision | What's my next step? |

Never answer tomorrow's question before today's.

---

# Emotional Curve

Visitors should experience this progression.

```
Chaos

↓

Relief

↓

Curiosity

↓

Possibility

↓

Confidence

↓

Excitement

↓

Commitment
```

Every page should move them forward.

Never backward.

---

# Common Mistakes

Never begin with:

Features.

AI.

Pricing.

Integrations.

Technical language.

Company history.

Visitors haven't earned that information yet.

Start with their world.

Not ours.

---

# Story Structure

Every page should reinforce the same story.

The contractor is the hero.

Their business has become more complicated than they expected.

Traditional software asks them to change.

TULBOXX adapts to them.

Their business becomes organized.

Their confidence grows.

Their company grows.

Everything points back to that story.

---

# Design Responsibility

Design supports psychology.

It should reduce uncertainty.

Not create decoration.

Animations explain.

Screenshots reassure.

Photography creates authenticity.

Whitespace creates clarity.

Everything has a purpose.

---

# Success

The website succeeds when visitors stop evaluating software...

...and start imagining a better business.

That moment changes everything.

After that point...

The demo becomes confirmation.

Not persuasion.

---

# The Ultimate Goal

The visitor should leave thinking:

"They understand exactly how I work."

"This feels like it was built for me."

"I finally found software that fits my business instead of forcing my business to fit the software."

That is the experience every page should create.
