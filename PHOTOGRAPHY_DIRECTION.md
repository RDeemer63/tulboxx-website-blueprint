# TULBOXX Photography Direction

**Version:** 1.0

**Status:** Canonical

**Applies to:** Generated photography, commissioned photography, campaign art direction, image selection, compositing, cropping, and post-processing for the TULBOXX marketing website.

---

# Purpose

Every TULBOXX photograph should feel as though it came from the same photographer, camera kit, editing process, and campaign.

The visual north star is:

> A very good commercial photographer quietly documenting a real contractor's day.

Photography should help contractors recognize their world. It must not make TULBOXX look like generic construction software, a stock-photo library, or a cinematic advertising campaign.

---

# Core Look

The imagery should feel like premium documentary commercial photography for the trades.

It should be:

- Natural
- Capable
- Observational
- Grounded
- Consistent

It should not be:

- Cinematic advertising
- Artificially gritty
- Generic stock photography
- Overly polished or staged

The photographer may be present on the jobsite, but should never appear to direct the contractor into an obvious pose.

---

# Camera Language

## Lens Character

Use a consistent full-frame documentary and editorial look.

- Use a 35mm visual character for environmental scenes.
- Use a 50mm visual character for closer contractor moments.
- Use roughly 35–40mm for wide homepage campaign scenes.
- Avoid ultra-wide distortion unless the environment cannot otherwise be explained.
- Avoid compressed telephoto portraiture that separates the person from the work.

## Camera Height and Perspective

- Work mostly at eye level.
- A slightly lower viewpoint is acceptable when equipment or work context needs emphasis.
- Never use dramatic low-angle construction-hero photography.
- Keep the contractor visibly connected to the environment.

## Depth of Field

Use moderate depth of field with a visual character similar to `f/4–f/5.6`.

The contractor should be immediately readable while the truck, equipment, property, and working conditions remain recognizable.

Avoid:

- Extreme portrait blur
- Artificially sharp backgrounds
- Everything-in-focus HDR rendering

The location is part of the story.

---

# Lighting

## Preferred Conditions

- Overcast daylight
- Light cloud cover
- Soft midday daylight
- Natural window or ambient light for truck and interior scenes

The goal is soft shadows, natural skin tones, and muted highlights. It should feel like a photographer arrived on a normal Tuesday.

## Avoid

- Golden-hour glow applied to every scene
- Orange-and-teal grading
- Heavy rim lighting
- Artificial sun rays
- Dramatic storm clouds
- Unrealistic backlighting
- Strong studio lighting on jobsites
- Blown-out skies

---

# Color and Texture

## Saturation

Keep saturation slightly muted.

- Dirt remains naturally brown.
- Grass remains believable.
- Skin stays neutral.
- Equipment colors may remain visible without artificial boosting.

## Contrast and Tonality

Use low-to-moderate contrast and preserve detail in dark clothing, truck paint, cloudy skies, dirt, and equipment.

- Highlights should remain soft.
- Shadows should stay open enough to retain information.
- Blacks should not be crushed for mood.
- White balance should be neutral to slightly cool.

## Sharpness

Favor real optical detail over digital sharpness.

Keep:

- Natural skin texture
- Dirt on boots
- Mud on trucks
- Fabric texture
- Scratches and ordinary wear

Avoid:

- HDR microcontrast
- Hyper-sharpened hair or beard detail
- Crispy clouds
- Every piece of gravel rendered with equal emphasis
- Unrealistically perfect pores, fabric, or machinery

Use this instruction in every generation prompt:

> Natural lens rendering, restrained sharpening, no HDR processing, and no exaggerated microcontrast.

---

# Composition

## Website-Aware Framing

People should rarely be centered.

- When copy appears on the left, weight the contractor and activity to the right.
- When copy appears on the right, weight the contractor and activity to the left.
- Treat negative space as an intentional layout area.
- Keep faces, hands, phones, and essential work context safe for responsive crops.
- Create a dedicated portrait composition when a desktop crop cannot produce a strong mobile image.

## Environmental Context

The contractor should exist inside the work rather than in front of it. Trucks, equipment, properties, weather, and site conditions should explain where the person is and why the moment matters.

Do not let equipment dominate every frame. It is context, not the hero.

---

# Body Language and Expression

## Preferred Actions

Show the contractor doing something real:

- Checking a phone
- Reviewing measurements
- Closing a truck door
- Talking with a customer
- Looking over a job
- Writing notes
- Walking toward equipment
- Reviewing a schedule from the truck
- Taking a project photo
- Looking over completed work

## Avoided Poses

- Arms crossed
- Crossed ankles
- Dramatic leaning and staring into the distance
- Smiling directly at the camera
- Thumbs up
- Handshakes
- Pointing at a laptop
- Generic contractor hero poses

The photographer observes. The photographer does not stage an advertisement.

## Emotional Range

The default expression is focused and calm. Acceptable secondary emotions include thinking, listening, reviewing, relaxed concentration, and slight satisfaction after completing work.

Avoid exaggerated frustration, joy, exhaustion, pride, or tough-guy seriousness. The contractor should look capable, not like a character.

# Homepage Campaign Character

Maintain one recognizable owner-operator throughout the homepage photography campaign.

## Character

- Male owner-operator in his late 30s or early 40s
- Short beard
- Practical haircut
- Average-to-solid working build
- Weathered without exaggeration
- Confident without looking styled
- Believable as an excavation contractor rather than a fitness model

Use the approved `HOME-HERO-01` image as the identity reference for later homepage generations. Do not regenerate a merely similar contractor when reference-based generation or editing is available.

## Wardrobe

- Charcoal or dark-gray hoodie or work shirt
- Tan or khaki work pants
- Worn brown work boots
- Dark neutral baseball cap
- Practical layers appropriate to the weather

Clothing should show normal use, slight dirt, and ordinary wear. It should never look perfectly clean or costume-level dirty.

## Branding

- Prefer blank clothing and vehicles.
- Do not generate business names, logos, phone numbers, or lettering.
- Composite approved TULBOXX or fictional campaign branding afterward only when it performs a clear communication job.

---

# Truck, Equipment, and Jobsite

## Truck

Use a dark charcoal or graphite heavy-duty pickup that is slightly muddy, used, maintained, and equipped for work.

Avoid showroom finishes, massive lift kits, excessive chrome, obvious luxury trim, and generated business lettering. The truck should communicate a successful contractor, not a truck influencer.

## Equipment

For the excavation campaign, prioritize:

- Compact excavator
- Skid steer or compact track loader
- Small-to-mid-sized contractor equipment

Equipment should be working, used, dirty, and maintained—never pristine dealership inventory.

## Jobsite

Show believable residential excavation work rather than large civil construction.

Preferred work includes:

- Drainage
- Grading
- Site preparation
- Driveways
- Retaining and site improvement
- Small clearing projects

This environment should remain consistent with the Morgan Lee drainage and grading demonstration story used across the product pages.

---

# Real-World Imperfection

Every photograph should include subtle evidence of real work, such as:

- Mud splash on a truck
- Tire marks
- Uneven gravel
- A small material pile
- A tool on the seat
- A water bottle
- Work gloves
- Slightly wrinkled clothing
- Dust
- Incomplete landscaping around active work

Imperfection should support credibility. Do not add clutter merely to make an image look busy.

---

# Image-Generation Standard

Every photography prompt should include the intent of the following master direction:

> Premium documentary commercial photography, natural environmental portrait, authentic contractor at a real residential jobsite, observational rather than posed, full-frame camera look, 35–50mm lens, moderate depth of field, soft overcast natural daylight, neutral color balance, restrained saturation, low-to-moderate contrast, natural skin texture, subtle real-world imperfections, realistic dirt and equipment wear, natural lens rendering, restrained sharpening, no HDR processing, no exaggerated microcontrast, no cinematic color grading, and no stock-photo posing.

Every prompt must also include:

> Do not generate company lettering, readable paperwork, app UI, logos, vehicle branding, fake customer information, or visible synthetic text.

Generate product UI separately from approved product captures. Composite it only after the underlying photograph is selected.

Generated photography may illustrate campaign scenarios and trade categories. It must never impersonate a real customer, testimonial, case study, employee, or documented customer result.

# Homepage Campaign Continuity

The first four homepage photographs should feel as though they occurred during one workday.

## `HOME-HERO-01`

Morning or midday. The contractor checks his phone beside the truck at an active residential jobsite.

## `HOME-REALITY-02`

During the day and early evening. The same contractor, wardrobe, truck, and project reveal recognizable administrative friction.

## `HOME-FIELD-07`

Midday. The same contractor uses TULBOXX between real tasks at the same project.

## `HOME-CTA-10`

Late afternoon or early evening. Work is wrapping up in natural light with a calmer composition and no dramatic sunset treatment.

The narrative arc is:

```text
Start the day
    ↓
Manage the chaos
    ↓
Stay organized
    ↓
Finish in control
```

---

# Post-Processing Standard

Apply one restrained treatment across the campaign:

- Exposure: natural
- Contrast: restrained
- Highlights: reduced slightly
- Shadows: opened slightly
- Whites: neutral
- Blacks: not crushed
- Texture: subtle
- Clarity: very low
- Dehaze: essentially none
- Vibrance: slightly reduced
- Saturation: neutral to slightly reduced
- Temperature: neutral to slightly cool
- Sharpening: restrained
- Grain: extremely subtle, if used

The target is not cinematic. It is consistent, believable documentary commercial photography.

---

# Production Deliverables

For every approved generated or commissioned photograph, preserve:

- High-resolution source
- Desktop composition
- Mobile-specific portrait composition or approved crop
- Web-optimized output
- Descriptive alt-text recommendation
- Original prompt
- Reference image identifiers
- Seed or reproducibility information when available
- Retouching and compositing notes
- Approval status

Do not place generated source files directly into production without crop, compression, metadata, accessibility, and brand review.

---

# Final Review

Before approving a photograph, ask:

1. Does this look observed rather than staged?
2. Could this plausibly happen during a normal contractor's workday?
3. Does the environment still explain the work?
4. Is the contractor capable without becoming a construction stereotype?
5. Does the lighting and color match the rest of the campaign?
6. Is there usable space for the intended page copy?
7. Will the essential story survive on mobile?
8. Is all generated text, branding, customer information, and product UI absent?
9. Does the image avoid implying that a generated person is a real customer?
10. Does it feel like a great photographer quietly documented the moment?

If not, revise or reject it.
