# Page Purpose

The Pricing page helps a visitor decide whether TULBOXX is worth the investment and whether to secure the current Ground Floor Rate.

By this point, the visitor should understand the philosophy, platform, workflow, features, and relevance. Pricing should reduce uncertainty—not restart the sales pitch.

The page must make the price, lifetime rate protection, included value, and next step easy to understand.

# Global Page Rules

- Publish exact pricing wherever possible.
- Explain billing frequency, taxes, fees, limits, and renewal clearly.
- Never invent plan names, prices, discounts, trial terms, or guarantees.
- Never hide essential differences in fine print.
- Never label a plan “Most Popular” without real evidence or a deliberate recommendation rationale.
- Use one primary CTA.
- Present one complete offer. Do not imply that Ground Floor customers receive a limited tier.
- Use modeled ROI only with visible assumptions and disclaimers.
- Answer real objections directly.
- Keep visual design restrained so the decision remains central.

---

# Pricing Data Contract

Maintain one approved source of truth containing:

- Public offer name
- Price, currency, and monthly billing period
- Active-subscription rate-protection rule
- Included users and usage limits when finalized
- All-feature entitlement
- Add-ons and fees
- Trial and eligibility rules, if introduced
- Enrollment close event when selected
- Cancellation and refund terms
- Regional restrictions
- Effective date
- Approving owner

The page, checkout, sales materials, and product must not contradict this source.

---

# Future Pricing Review - Internal Planning Only

Do not publish future plan names, tiers, or prices before V2 and the relevant roadmap are reviewed.

When Ground Floor enrollment is ready to close:

1. Inventory the capabilities completed in V2.
2. Review the near-term roadmap and cost-to-serve.
3. Identify genuinely different customer needs by company stage, users, usage, or advanced capability.
4. Decide whether one straightforward standard plan still serves customers best.
5. Create tiers only when each tier has a clear customer, value boundary, and reason to exist.
6. Validate pricing against customer value and willingness to pay.
7. Set and communicate the Ground Floor close event before standard pricing begins.

Existing Ground Floor customers retain their $39 monthly rate and all-feature access for the life of their active subscription when standard pricing changes.

# Accessibility, Mobile, and Performance

- Use one semantic `h1` and logical headings.
- Announce any dynamic price or checkout changes accessibly.
- Do not communicate offer conditions through color alone.
- Make included-feature groups and accordions keyboard accessible.
- Show complete price and billing units near every CTA.
- Avoid a wide tier-comparison table while only one offer exists.
- Keep calculator inputs properly labeled and validated.
- Use minimal JavaScript and avoid heavy media.
- Ensure the decision remains possible if interactive enhancements fail.

---

# Page Acceptance Criteria

The page is ready when:

- Every price and term is approved.
- Visitors understand the $39 Ground Floor Rate within seconds.
- Billing units and commitments are unambiguous.
- Fees and meaningful limits are disclosed.
- Included-feature data matches the product and checkout.
- ROI assumptions are transparent and defensible.
- FAQs answer real objections directly.
- Mobile offer details are usable.
- Ground Floor terms match checkout and subscription behavior.
- The page creates confidence without pressure.

---

# Final Test

The page should not leave the visitor thinking:

> I need to talk to someone just to understand what this costs.

It should leave them thinking:

> I understand the investment, the value, and exactly what to do next.
