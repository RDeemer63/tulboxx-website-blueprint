# Page Purpose

This page sells the philosophy behind TULBOXX before selling the software.

It should explain why running a growing service business becomes unnecessarily difficult, make clear that the contractor is not the problem, and establish the beliefs that guide the product.

The visitor should leave understanding that TULBOXX is different because it starts with how contractors naturally work—not with how traditional business software expects them to work.

## Editorial Ownership

This page owns the TULBOXX product philosophy: software should serve the work, natural workflows should come first, structure should grow with the job, the field sets the standard, and contractor judgment remains in control. It should acknowledge the administrative burden established on Home without retelling the full recognition story.

# Section 2 - The Job Nobody Started For

## Stage

Seen → Understood

## Question Answered

How did running the business become so demanding?

## Copy

**Eyebrow**

The second job.

**Headline**

At some point, doing the work became running the work.

**Body copy**

Growth creates more customers, decisions, and responsibility around every job.

But most growing contractors do not suddenly gain an office team, more hours in the day, or a second brain that remembers every detail.

When the systems underneath the work do not grow with the business, the contractor absorbs the difference. More gets carried in memory. More gets finished at night. More depends on one person keeping the whole company moving.

That is not a work-ethic problem. It is a systems problem.

## Visual Direction

**Asset ID:** `WHY-SECOND-JOB-02`

**Purpose**

Make the invisible administrative workload visible.

**Format**

A documentary-style sequence following one workday.

**Moments**

Use one short transition from active fieldwork to the business responsibility created by that same job. Do not repeat the homepage's full burden sequence.

**Direction**

Use one contractor throughout the sequence. Continuity matters more than showing many trades. The viewer should feel one day's responsibilities accumulating.

**Motion option**

A time-of-day progression may reveal the moments from morning to evening. The sequence must remain clear without motion.

## Exit Condition

The visitor should think:

> They understand the part of the business nobody talks about.

# Section 3 - The False Choice

## Stage

Understood → Relieved

## Question Answered

Do I really have to choose between doing the work and managing the business?

## Copy

**Eyebrow**

Growth should create opportunity.

**Headline**

You should not have to choose between the field and the business.

**Body copy**

Most business software offers the same answer to growth: add more fields, more stages, more rules, and more time maintaining the system.

That leaves the contractor with a false choice—stay close to the work and risk losing control of the business, or stay on top of the software and spend less time doing the work that built the company.

You should be able to grow without choosing between the field and the business. And without becoming a full-time software administrator.

## Editorial Boundary

Home owns the full current-state and better-future contrast. Keep this section focused on the false choice created by software that demands administration.

## Visual Direction

**Asset ID:** `WHY-FALSE-CHOICE-03`

**Purpose**

Show that the conflict between fieldwork and administration is created by broken systems, not by a lack of effort.

**Format**

A split narrative using authentic work moments rather than an abstract balance scale.

**Concept**

One side shows visible progress on a real job. The other shows the business tasks created by that same job. A restrained connector makes clear that both belong to one workflow.

**Avoid**

- “Work-life balance” stock photography
- Family imagery used as emotional pressure
- Shaming language
- Red warning graphics or alarm icons

## Exit Condition

The visitor should think:

> The problem is the way the work is being managed—not my ability to work hard.

# Section 4 - The Problem Is Not the Contractor

## Stage

Relieved → Aligned

## Question Answered

Why does traditional business software feel like more work?

## Copy

**Eyebrow**

The problem is not you.

**Headline**

Software got the relationship backwards.

**Body copy**

You already know how to talk to a customer, walk a property, understand the work, and know when a job is complete.

The trouble starts when software asks you to stop working naturally and start maintaining the software.

Create a record. Update a pipeline. Copy the details. Convert the estimate. Change another status. Enter the same information again.

The contractor should not have to learn the software's workflow.

The software should understand the contractor's workflow.

## Principle Statement

**Software should serve the work. The work should never serve the software.**

## Visual Direction

**Asset ID:** `WHY-BACKWARDS-04`

**Purpose**

Explain rigid software friction without showing a competitor or turning the section into a product comparison.

**Format**

A simple interaction diagram.

**Concept**

Show a natural customer conversation branching into unnecessary duplicate records and repeated entry. Then show the same conversation remaining connected as the work moves forward.

**Design rule**

The “backwards” path should feel cumbersome because of visible repetition, not because it is covered in red error states. The TULBOXX path should feel calmer because information remains connected.

## Exit Condition

The visitor should think:

> I should not have to become a software expert to run my business.

# Section 5 - How Work Actually Happens

## Stage

Aligned

## Question Answered

What does it mean to build software around the contractor?

## Copy

**Eyebrow**

Start where the work starts.

**Headline**

Real work usually begins with a conversation.

**Body copy**

A customer calls. A text comes in. Someone sends a picture. You walk the property. A conversation happens in the driveway.

Someone asks, “What would it cost to fix this?”

That conversation is the beginning of the work.

Everything after it should grow naturally from the information already captured.

## Natural Work Pattern

```text
Talk to the customer
        ↓
Understand the work
        ↓
Organize what matters
        ↓
Quote it when needed
        ↓
Do the job
        ↓
Invoice appropriately
        ↓
Get paid
```

## Closing Copy

TULBOXX creates structure underneath that process without asking you to become a software administrator.

## Visual Direction

**Asset ID:** `WHY-NATURAL-WORK-05`

**Purpose**

Show that TULBOXX begins with familiar contractor behavior rather than a software screen.

**Format**

A real-world workflow anchored by photography, with restrained product detail appearing only after the conversation.

**Sequence**

1. Customer and contractor discuss the work on site.
2. Contractor captures a voice note, photo, or rough description.
3. The information becomes an organized project record.
4. The contractor chooses the next step.

**Rule**

Do not begin this sequence with a dashboard. Begin with people and work.

## Exit Condition

The visitor should think:

> This starts the way my work starts.

# Section 6 - What We Believe

## Stage

Aligned → Hopeful

## Question Answered

What principles guide the product?

## Intro Copy

**Eyebrow**

Our product beliefs.

**Headline**

Good software should make you feel more capable.

**Body copy**

Every product decision should remove friction, protect the contractor's judgment, and make the business easier to run.

## Belief 1

**Simple work should stay simple.**

A quick service call should not require the same process as a long commercial project. Structure should appear when it helps and stay out of the way when it does not.

## Belief 2

**Do not make the contractor rebuild what the business already knows.**

If the business already knows the address, scope, photos, price, or schedule, the contractor should not have to recreate it in another tool.

## Belief 3

**Incomplete information is allowed.**

Real work develops over time. Start with what is known and add detail when it becomes useful.

## Belief 4

**AI organizes. Contractors decide.**

Technology can help turn rough information into a clear next step. It should never invent scope, set a price, or approve work for the contractor.

## Belief 5

**Professionalism should not require an office staff.**

A solo contractor or small team should be able to communicate clearly, follow up consistently, and present work professionally.

## Belief 6

**The field wins. Every time.**

Software used in trucks, on jobsites, in bad reception, and between appointments must respect limited time and attention.

## Visual Direction

**Asset ID:** `WHY-BELIEFS-06`

**Purpose**

Turn the product philosophy into concrete, memorable decisions.

**Format**

Six editorial belief panels. Each panel pairs one principle with one real-world consequence.

**Design direction**

- Avoid generic icon cards.
- Use close details: one-handed mobile use, an estimate preview, a voice note becoming a draft, a simple job versus a complex project.
- Allow the belief statements to carry the visual hierarchy.
- Keep motion limited to explaining cause and effect.

## Exit Condition

The visitor should think:

> These are the same things I wish business software understood.

**Eyebrow**

The field wins.

**Headline**

We are building for the person with five minutes before the next appointment.

The primary experience is not someone sitting behind three monitors.

# Visual System for This Page

## Canonical Reference

Use [`docs/TULBOXX_BRAND_STYLE_GUIDE.pdf`](docs/TULBOXX_BRAND_STYLE_GUIDE.pdf) for brand colors, typography, logo treatment, and overall visual character.

Use [`PHOTOGRAPHY_DIRECTION.md`](PHOTOGRAPHY_DIRECTION.md) for all photographic art direction, generation, selection, cropping, and post-processing.

## Page Character

This page should feel:

- Human before technical
- Honest before polished
- Calm rather than dramatic
- Industrial without becoming gritty decoration
- Optimistic without becoming sentimental

## Visual Rhythm

1. Begin with immersive real-work photography.
2. Use the middle of the page for clear contrast and belief structures.
3. Return to human outcomes before the closing statement.
4. Introduce only enough product UI to prove that the philosophy affects the product.

## Photography Rules

- Use specific trades, real tools, and credible work conditions.
- Preserve dignity and competence in every portrayal.
- Show administrative friction without staging incompetence.
- Maintain continuity when one contractor appears across a sequence.
- Never use generated people as customer proof.
- Never generate readable product UI, logos, paperwork, or customer text inside photography.

## Typography Direction

- Rajdhani Bold for high-impact belief statements and section headlines.
- Poppins for body copy, navigation, supporting labels, and actions.
- Keep manifesto-style lines short and readable rather than oversized for effect.

## Motion Direction

- Use motion to show accumulation, connection, and cause-and-effect.
- Do not animate every belief panel.
- Keep scrolling predictable.
- Provide static fallbacks and respect reduced-motion preferences.

# Mobile Experience

- Keep the full hero belief understandable before the first large image.
- Break long philosophical copy into short, intentional reading beats.
- Preserve the one-day sequence as a vertical story.
- Use progressive disclosure for the six beliefs without hiding them behind hover.
- Keep the natural-work pattern legible without requiring horizontal scrolling.
- Make Explore the Platform available after the hero and at the close.
- Avoid sticky elements that compete with the emotional copy.

---

# Accessibility Requirements

- Use one semantic `h1` for the hero headline.
- Preserve logical heading hierarchy.
- Provide descriptive alt text for real-work photography.
- Do not encode the false-choice or natural-work relationships through color alone.
- Make belief panels keyboard accessible if interactive.
- Provide static equivalents for scroll-driven sequences.
- Maintain WCAG AA contrast at minimum.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Keep line lengths comfortable and allow text resizing without clipping.

---

# Performance Requirements

- Prioritize the hero copy before loading secondary sequence imagery.
- Use responsive images and stable aspect-ratio containers.
- Lazy-load documentary images below the fold.
- Avoid video when a short image sequence communicates the story.
- Reuse approved visual assets where doing so strengthens continuity.
- Test scroll interactions on mid-range mobile devices.

# Page Acceptance Criteria

The page is ready for production when:

- It explains why TULBOXX exists without becoming a company-history page.
- The contractor remains capable and respected throughout the story.
- The enemy is clearly unnecessary friction, not another software company.
